The ledger

We did not invent the ledger.
We made it derive itself.

Double-entry bookkeeping is seven hundred years old. What is new is that the ledger is backed by provenance: its entries are derived from a record of what actually happened, by one mechanism, whatever is being counted.

Anchored

Every entry names the acts that justify it, so “why is this figure here?” always has an answer.

Conserved before written

An entry must balance under that resource's own law, or it is refused. You are never handed a figure that was quietly patched.

Restated, never edited

A changed factor reverses the prior statement and posts a new dated version. Last year's number stays last year's number.

Independently checkable

The books reconcile against the account your own systems keep: two accounts of the same events, agreeing or disagreeing loudly.

One mechanism, many books: money, stock, space in m²·day, carbon in kg CO₂e, and the sales funnel.

A light touch

For the business

Nobody types anything new. No new screens, no new fields, no change to how anyone works.

For IT

Read-only. No schema change, nothing written back. The record lives in your own PostgreSQL, read with SQL.

Open foundations

Your record, in open standards

No proprietary format anywhere: not the database, not the query language, not the export.

  • W3C PROV The web standard for provenance.
  • PROV-JSONLD A compact, linked-data serialisation of PROV.
  • Provenance templates The record is generated from declared templates, so its meaning is published, not buried in code.
  • OpenProv The OpenProvenance extensions to PROV.
  • ProvToolbox Open-source Java toolkit for PROV and provenance templates.
  • PostgreSQL and SQL The record sits in your own database, readable by the tools you already own.
